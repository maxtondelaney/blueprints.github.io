    Concept framing layout. Final member sizing, connections, shear/braced-wall requirements, post bases, and foundation load paths require code/engineering verification. This is not a stamped structural drawing.
    This wall is a bearing wall. Like the left wall and unlike the front and rear gable walls, the right wall carries the rafters: each rafter bears on its double top plate with a 3-1/2" birdsmouth seat, and the tributary load is half the 12'-0" roof span, so roughly 6'-0" of roof depth per foot of wall. There is no opening in this wall, so every rafter lands over continuous full-height framing and no header is involved anywhere on the sheet.
    Assumed load path (REQUIRES STRUCTURAL REVIEW — NOT ENGINEER APPROVED): 2×8 rafters → birdsmouth seat on double 2×6 top plate → 2×6 studs and three-stud 2×6 framed corners → continuous pressure-treated bottom plate → slab. The corner studs land on that same plate rather than on the slab. Uplift is resisted by a hurricane / rafter tie at every rafter, and at the base by hold-downs at the corners.

    Corners: the four corners are standard three-stud 2×6 framed corners rather than solid 6×6 posts. The corner block still takes up 5-1/2" of wall length, because that is the thickness of the wall returning at right angles, so nothing in the horizontal layout moved: this wall still totals 120", the 109" of stud infill between the corners is unchanged, the 16" module measured from the front face is unchanged, and the sheathed pier is still the full wall length. Inside the block sit a 2×6 on edge at each end and a 2×6 laid flat at the interior face between them, backing the drywall return on the gable wall, with the void behind the flat member left for insulation. One consequence is worth naming: the pier chord at each corner is now three separate 2×6 instead of one solid post, so those members have to be nailed or bolted together to act as one before a hold-down can develop tension in them. A single 2×6 will not. Ganging and hold-down attachment are per engineer. There is no 6×6 anywhere on this wall now — no openings, no header, no posts — so the framed corners are the only chord locations it has.
    Roof shown beyond: the roof slopes up and away from this wall at 6:12, so it projects edge-on in this view as the light dashed area above the plate. The 12" eave overhang projects toward the viewer and is not a length on this sheet; the 12" rake overhang at each gable extends the roof 12" past each end of this wall.
    Sheathing: the building gets a full plywood wrap — every wall sheathed in 21/32" (5/8" nominal) plywood, and the roof deck the same panel. The wall sheathing is noted rather than drawn, because in an elevation the panel faces the viewer and has no visible thickness; the roof deck is edge-on here, so its 21/32" shows up only in the top-of-sheathing elevations quoted for the roof beyond. Worth being clear about what this does and does not buy: 21/32" is thicker than the 7/16" or 15/32" normally run for shear, so panel shear capacity is not what constrains this building. The constraints are pier length and aspect ratio, and a thicker panel does not relax either rule — it does not make this wall's favourable aspect ratio count for more, and it does nothing for the walls that are over the limit. Span rating, edge nailing, blocking and the bracing schedule are per engineer.
